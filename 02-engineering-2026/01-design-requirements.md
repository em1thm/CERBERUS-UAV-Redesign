# Initial UAV Sizing and Thrust Requirements

*CERBERUS Engineering Redesign — 2026*

## Establishing the first design parameters

The original CERBERUS illustration from 2021 showed an eight-rotor aircraft, but our presentation did not include calculations for its propulsion system.

When returning to the project in 2026, I wanted to establish a starting point for the mechanical design: how much thrust would each motor need to produce, and how would the aircraft's total mass affect that requirement?

I began with a simplified hover calculation, using a provisional takeoff mass of 3.2 kg. This provided an initial reference before selecting specific motors, propellers or structural components.

## Reference configuration

| Parameter | Initial value |
|---|---|
| Configuration | Flat octocopter |
| Number of rotors | 8 |
| Assumed takeoff mass | 3.2 kg |
| Gravitational acceleration | 9.81 m/s² |
| Target static thrust-to-weight ratio | 2:1 |
| Flight condition | Stationary hover, no wind |

The 3.2 kg mass was a working estimate established before preparing a component-level mass budget.

A 2:1 static thrust-to-weight ratio was selected as an initial sizing target. It provides a basis for evaluating motor–propeller combinations with additional thrust capacity for manoeuvring and control.

Biczyski et al. (2020) describe this type of preliminary thrust-based sizing in their multirotor design methodology, together with the importance of propeller selection, operating conditions and endurance estimation [1].

## Hover thrust calculation

During stationary hover, the total vertical thrust generated by the rotors must balance the aircraft's weight.

The weight is calculated using:

**W = m × g**

For the initial configuration:

W = 3.2 × 9.81

**W = 31.392 N**

Assuming equal thrust distribution across eight rotors:

**T_hover = W / N**

T_hover = 31.392 / 8

**T_hover = 3.924 N per motor**

For comparison with motor manufacturer specifications, the thrust can be expressed in gram-force.

Using 1 gf = 0.00980665 N [2]:

T_hover = 3.924 / 0.00980665

**T_hover ≈ 400 gf per motor**

The preliminary maximum static thrust target follows from the selected 2:1 thrust-to-weight ratio:

T_max = 2 × T_hover

**T_max ≈ 800 gf per motor**

This gives an initial reference for evaluating the propulsion system.

## Revising the mass estimate

The next stage of the investigation involved selecting reference propulsion and electronic components and estimating the remaining structural mass.

The preliminary mass budget, documented separately, produced a total of **3,535 g**.

This represents an increase of approximately **10.47%** over the initial 3.2 kg assumption.

Using the revised mass:

W = 3.535 × 9.81

**W = 34.678 N**

With eight motors sharing the load equally:

T_hover = 34.678 / 8

**T_hover ≈ 4.335 N ≈ 442 gf per motor**

Maintaining the preliminary 2:1 thrust-to-weight target gives:

**T_max ≈ 884 gf per motor**

### Effect of aircraft mass on thrust requirements

| Takeoff mass | Hover thrust / motor | Maximum static thrust target / motor |
|---|---:|---:|
| 3.200 kg | 400 gf | 800 gf |
| 3.535 kg | 442 gf | 884 gf |
| 4.000 kg | 500 gf | 1,000 gf |

All three cases assume eight equally loaded rotors and a target thrust-to-weight ratio of 2:1.

The relationship is approximately linear: increasing the aircraft mass increases the required hover thrust by the same percentage.

## Implications for the redesign

The revised calculation establishes approximately **442 gf of hover thrust per motor** as the current reference requirement for the octocopter.

The next propulsion selection should consider the thrust available from a complete motor–propeller combination at the intended battery voltage.

Motor efficiency, propeller diameter, electrical power consumption and aircraft mass are closely connected. Experimental propulsion research by Goli et al. (2023) examines these relationships using different motor, propeller and battery combinations [3].

The current calculation assumes steady, level hover with equal thrust distribution. It establishes a force requirement that can be carried forward into more detailed propulsion analysis. Flight dynamics, rotor interactions, motor thermal limits, battery voltage variation and manoeuvring performance will be considered during later design stages.

For CERBERUS, I will also need to account for the sensing payload, which may influence both the total mass and the geometry of the aircraft.

The updated mass estimate will therefore serve as the starting point for the [component-level propulsion budget](03-mass-and-propulsion-budget.md) and subsequent Fusion 360 modelling.
